Beyond Global Rank Statistics: Localized Certification for Quantile-Thresholded Decision Systems

Second author, under review
  • Ranking
  • Certification
  • Robustness

Systems that threshold a score at a quantile, from CFAR detection to selective classification, are usually certified with global rank statistics. Those average over the whole score distribution, but the threshold acts at a single point.

The problem

As a result, the certificate can look nearly perfect while the decisions change underneath it. In one input-noise case at a 5% budget, the Spearman rank correlation stays at 0.9885, yet the selected set’s Jaccard churn is 0.628: about 46% of the selected inputs are replaced.

The idea

An input’s decision changes only when it crosses the threshold. So instead of rank agreement across all inputs, the paper measures how far ranks move near the threshold: the standard deviation of rank displacement in a window around the operating quantile, with a half-width of 0.02 in normalized rank.

Results

The study spans 3,570 operating points across thirteen perturbation families. In the small-perturbation regime, where churn stays below 0.15, the local displacement scale predicts selected-set churn with R² = 0.968, against 0.655 for the global control. The advantage is largest when the budget is tight.

Caveats

This is an empirical fit with a first-order approximation, not a universal law. It assumes small perturbations and regular local behavior, and the advantage shrinks for wider budgets and once churn saturates. It does not replace the usual checks either: accuracy and task quality still need testing, and the actual selected-set overlap should be measured at the intended budget.
